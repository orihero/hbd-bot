# The concept dossier — twenty ideas and what the judges did to them

**Frozen at 2026-09-16.** The working papers behind
[`01-instagram-launch-playbook.md`](01-instagram-launch-playbook.md). Twenty concepts were generated by
five creative directors working different angles, and each was judged three times by reviewers
instructed to **refute** it rather than assess it: **realism** (can it really reach a million with an
Uzbek audience), **producible** (can it be shot tomorrow inside Higgsfield's real limits),
**risk/conversion** (does it endanger the brand, and does a viewer who loves it ever type `/start`).

This file is kept for one reason: **the objections are more reusable than the concepts.** Most of them
apply to any video this brand shoots. Read the objection column before pitching anything new.

### The hardest objection raised against each

Kept because the objections are more useful than the scores — several of them apply to any video this
brand shoots, not only to the concept they were aimed at.

**«Gulya emas»** *(name-pronunciation, mean 5.7)*

- *realism 5/10, rework* — The share economy is inverted, and the concept's own "whyItSpreads" admits it: "a send here is an apology with a gift attached." Nobody forwards a confession of their own rudeness. You are asking a man to DM his colleague of ten years a 50-second film whose thesis is "you have been humiliating this woman since 2016, now pay 15,000 soʻm to fix it." That send costs the sender social standing, and people share to make themselves look good — which means the only person with a clean reason to share is the victim, and the victim is not your buyer. Worse, the buyer you actually have is the 67%-male, 18–24 Instagram cohort, and a 21-year-old named Aziz in Ferghana has never been called anything but Aziz; he has no wound, no guilt, and no one to forward this to. Stack on top: the hook is a woman's face with explicitly NO text for the first 1.2 seconds while ~half the feed watches muted, so the premise is invisible at the exact frame skip rate is measured; the first 22 seconds have no audio bed at all, which is a documentary pacing decision made in a feed that punishes it; and the entire video stakes a public claim on the one thing every engine in the category demonstrably fails at — Uzbek name pronunciation — in front of the world's only qualified judges, who will find the 15% it gets wrong and put it in the comments. This is a good short film that will be admired by 60,000 people and bought by 300. The ceiling of "people who feel the Gulya wound AND are on Uzbek Instagram AND would forward it" is not a million.
  - Fix: Flip the addressee of the send from perpetrator to fellow-sufferer. Change one line of CTA — from "send this to the person whose name you shorten" to "Sizning ismingizni ham qisqartirishadimi? Toʻliq yozing" — and put the struck-through "Gulya → Gulchehra" card on frame 0 instead of 0:01.2. That converts the video from an accusation you forward downward into an identity flag you post about yourself, which is the only send mechanic that compounds, and it makes the premise legible with the sound off in under half a second. Everything else is optimisation; this is the difference between a guilt video and a participation format.
- *producible 7/10, rework* — The one Higgsfield shot in this video is the only thing that can destroy it, and it is decorative. A 4-second generated magenta flame dropped into a 50-second documentary piece whose entire credibility claim is «kameradagi yuzlar aktyor emas» is a self-inflicted wound: the viewer who clocks one synthetic frame at 0:22 retroactively doubts all six faces, including the six-second stunned silence you are betting the whole video on. And it is AI-obvious in the worst way — fire is the single hardest thing for these models to make physical, a 4s Arc Right around a flame in a dark room will read as plasticky looping light, and the camera-preset move stamps "made with an AI preset" on it. You are spending credits, minutes and your authenticity to buy a mood beat that a real candle on the same phone gives you for free. Separately, the real tomorrow-fails risk is nowhere near Higgsfield: it is six real people with genuinely butchered names, in one city block, in three hours, each reacting once and only once, filmed by two people on one phone — and six songs that must pronounce Xurshidabegim, Bibisora and Muhammadyusuf correctly on a stack the research itself says gets Uzbek "sometimes strange". The Higgsfield half of this concept is flawless because it barely uses Higgsfield. The human half has no coverage and no plan B.
  - Fix: Delete the Higgsfield shot. Replace 0:22–0:26 with a real flame (gas ring, candle, lighter) shot on the same phone in the same handheld grammar, or just 1.5s of black with room tone. That is a 20-minute change, costs zero credits, removes the only AI tell in the video, and makes the "not actors, not edited" claim unimpeachable.
- *risk-conversion 5/10, rework* — This is an apology video, not an ad. The send it earns — and it will earn plenty — travels from the guilty colleague to the person whose name got shortened. Neither end of that forward has been told to open Telegram, and the one receiving it is the gift recipient, not the buyer. Meanwhile the product is on screen for three seconds at 0:47, after the emotional peak has already passed at 0:30, and @bayram_uzbot is never once spoken aloud in fifty seconds. A viewer who cries at this has no memory of where to go. You have engineered the strongest send-trigger in the entire research pack and aimed it at a dead end: high sends-per-reach, near-zero /start. Second objection, and this one can drop the concept under 3 on its own: the caption makes two absolute factual claims — «reaksiya montaj qilinmagan» and that the bot checks the spelling and the stress and re-sings when it comes out wrong. If that verification-and-re-sing loop does not exist in the product today, that is not puffery, it is a specific capability claim made in writing about the exact thing the video exists to prove, aimed at an audience that will instantly test it with the hardest name they know — in a market eight weeks into a paid-greeting scam wave and primed to shout «firibgar» at the first mangled Xurshidabegim.
  - Fix: Two cards and one caption line, no re-shoot: put the chat screen with the visible @bayram_uzbot handle into the 0:26-0:28 earbud shot so the bot is named at the emotional peak instead of after it, and replace the final card with a comment-to-DM keyword plus the preview promise — «Izohga ISM yozing → 30 soniya bepul eshitasiz. 15 000 soʻm · @bayram_uzbot» — with the auto-DM carrying the raw t.me/bayram_uzbot?start=ig_gulya link inside its single permitted message. In the same pass, delete the stress-checking / re-singing sentence from the caption unless that loop has already shipped.

**BUVIM ISMINI QOʻSHIQDA ESHITDI** *(reaction-emotion, mean 5.7)*

- *realism 5/10, rework* — Seconds 2 to 7 are a 79-year-old woman doing nothing, and with the sound off nothing has happened at all — so the video deliberately places five seconds of dead air inside the exact window where roughly half of all viewers leave, and for the ~50% watching muted the entire payload (a name inside a lyric) is inaudible, leaving them a slow wide of an old lady and two lines of Uzbek exposition to read. "Do not cut the silence" is advice lifted from a 2021 North American PAID TikTok campaign, where the impression was bought; here the impression has to be earned against a cold-start test pool that reads skip rate. Worse, the concept's own best mechanic works against it on the metric that matters: the cousin-group-chat forward in Uzbekistan happens on Telegram, where 70-85% of the country lives, not in Instagram DMs — so the share that makes this concept smart produces zero sends-per-reach and Instagram never sees the signal that would push it past 100k. On top of that the addressable pool is inverted: Uzbek Instagram is 67.5% male and peaks at 18-24, and a slow Shashmaqom grandmother piece is the one genre that cohort scrolls past fastest. Grandmother-reaction is also not a pattern interrupt in this market; it is a saturated local genre, and the "AI" corner label at 0:13 converts the whole thing into a visibly branded ad at the emotional peak.
  - Fix: Make the name visible, not just audible, and delete the dead zone. Burn the sung lyric in as large karaoke-style Uzbek text so "OʻGʻILOY" lands as a full-screen hit at 0:01 — a muted viewer must see the name arrive in the song before they see her react. Then cut the held silence from five seconds to about two and a half by intercutting the granddaughter's face inside the same room and the same take (still one continuous audio bed, so nothing reads as staged) and move the pull-back wide to the tail. That is the smallest change that puts something happening in every second of 0:00-0:07 for both sound-on and sound-off viewers.
- *producible 8/10, ship* — The only Higgsfield shot in this video is a cutaway that breaks the exact thing the video is selling. Shot 2 declares "SAME TAKE, NO CUT — cutting it is what makes reaction videos read as staged," and then shot 5 cuts the take at 0:13 for three seconds of generated courtyard, after which shot 6 claims to be "back on her, unbroken." It isn't unbroken; you cut it yourself. Worse, you burn "Bu kadr — AI" on screen at second 13 — the precise moment a viewer is deciding whether any of this is real — so you have paid ~14 credits to manufacture your own credibility problem inside the one genre where the single fatal tell is an edit. And the shot is technically the worst three seconds in the file: a slow tilt up through a grape trellis is high-frequency foliage under continuous camera motion, which is where video models boil and crawl; at the specified 720p it is also the softest, lowest-resolution frame in an otherwise clean 1080p phone video, upscaled in CapCut and sitting right between two sharp handheld shots that expose it. Everything else on this lens is close to flawless — one generation, no lipsync, no singing avatar, no faces to keep consistent, no Uzbek glyph inside a generated frame, ~15 minutes of wall-clock generation, two people and one phone — which is precisely why the single AI shot is pure downside. The other reason this fails tomorrow has nothing to do with Higgsfield: the whole shoot is gated tonight on an AI singing "Oʻgʻiloy," a two-ʻ name in the one language the research says the entire stack mispronounces, with no bounded reroll count and no backup name.
  - Fix: Delete shot 5. Zero generations, zero credits, zero AI label, and the take stays genuinely unbroken — hold on her for the full 0:10–0:21 instead. If the AI beat is required for the honesty angle, move it after the end card as a 1s tag rather than into the middle of the reaction. If it must stay where it is, then generate it at 1080p 9:16 (not 720p), render 5s and trim to 3, and replace the grape trellis with a low-detail subject — sunlight crawling up a plastered wall, a doira resting on a korpacha, steam off a choynak — because a flat surface under a Tilt Up shows no artifacts and foliage always will.
- *risk-conversion 4/10, rework* — The only thing this video asks anyone to do is enter a lottery for a FREE song. "Buvingizning ismini izohga yozing — eng qiyinlariga bugun kechqurun qoʻshiq qilamiz" is not a CTA, it is a giveaway. If this lands 1M views you get thousands of grandmother names, you fulfil ten, and you teach Uzbek Instagram that Bayram hands out songs in the comments — spending your single best emotional asset to acquire an audience selected for not paying. The price appears nowhere: not on screen, not in the caption, not in the CTA. It is not sold honestly or dishonestly, it is absent. And the brand does not exist until 0:24 of a 26-second video, in a category whose own benchmark (Songfinch) is 6 seconds of average watch time — four out of five viewers who cry at this never learn who made it. Three problems compound it. (1) The caption makes a false product claim: "Bot ismni qayta-qayta tekshirib, toʻgʻri aytguncha qaytadan yozadi" describes an automated pronunciation-verification loop, while your own production note says a human listens and re-rolls. Uzbek ʻ/gʻ is documented as the exact place every AI in the stack fails, so this promise breaks on your first paying customer with an unusual name. (2) You label the harmless 3-second courtyard B-roll "AI" while the song — the thing that made her cry — is also AI and is labelled nothing. That is pointing at the decoy, and it is precisely the Songfinch trap: claim human authorship, collect "you lied" reviews. (3) A 79-year-old who is not on Instagram, deliberately not briefed, is filmed and her given name and face published as commercial creative, with "if she does not react, ship a different grandmother" as the casting note. One sentence fixes it, but as written the script engineers around her consent rather than obtaining it, and "Bayram exploited somebody's buvi" outruns any Reel. Finally, the spread logic contradicts the price: nobody convenes a cousin group chat to split a taxi fare.
  - Fix: Move the brand and the price to where the eyeballs are, and stop giving the product away. Cut the 0:13–0:16 Higgsfield courtyard insert entirely — it is the only unreal frame, it forces the half-disclosure, and it buys nothing — and use those 3 seconds to put a small persistent corner lockup on screen from 0:06: "@bayram_uzbot · 15 000 soʻm". Then change one word in the caption CTA: "izohga yozing" (free lottery) becomes "botga yozing" with the t.me deep link, so the action a moved viewer takes is opening the bot at a known price instead of queueing for charity.

**Buvimning ismi hech qayerda yoʻq** *(name-pronunciation, mean 5.3)*

- *realism 5/10, rework* — The entire video rests on a factual claim that is false and will be refuted in the comments within an hour: you can get any Uzbek name engraved on a keychain, a mug or a phone case in five minutes at essentially any bazaar stall or from the dozens of Uzbek Instagram gift shops that already sell exactly that (the research names @sovgala_podarki and @mushtari_giftbox, both of which do portraits and personalised cases). "Buvim 74 yil yashadi. Ismi hech qayerda yozilmagan" is not a heartbreaking fact — it is a fact about one rack in one kiosk, stated as a fact about the world, and the top comment will be "aka, 20 ming so'mga o'ydirib berishadi." The moment that comment lands, the emotional payload inverts: the video stops being about a woman erased and becomes about a brand that didn't check, and the refutation itself names a cheaper competitor to the product on your own post. Worse, choosing O'g'iloy — an old, rural-coded name — hands the audience the crueller version: "of course there's no O'g'iloy keychain, who would buy one?" The joke turns on the grandmother. And beneath the premise risk sits an ordinary craft failure: the first 1.5 seconds are a hand on a rack with no text, no anomaly and an off-camera Uzbek question, so a muted cold-start viewer gets nothing until 0:04 and the real idea does not arrive until the 0:10 overlay — on a surface where half of viewers are gone by 0:03. Then at 0:14 you cut to five seconds of obviously stylised AI b-roll in the middle of a video whose only currency is that it is documentary-true. That is a self-inflicted wound placed exactly at the retention cliff, and it is the frame where the piece announces itself as an ad. Finally the send logic is muddled: the CTA tells the viewer to send the video to the person whose name is missing, but grandmothers are not the Instagram audience, and "your name is on nothing" is a strange thing to forward to your mother.
  - Fix: Stop claiming scarcity about the world and claim it about the frame, where it is unfalsifiable and stronger. Replace the "0 ta" overlays and the "hech qayerda yozilmagan" line with a counter the footage itself proves: cold-open at 0:00 on the spinning rack with a burned-in card reading "47 ta ism. 3 tasi o'zbekcha." Nobody can refute a count of what is visibly on the rack, the number is a better hook than a vendor's "Yo'q," it lands in frame one for a muted viewer, and it reframes the piece from a sentimental error into a verifiable observation about whose names get manufactured.
- *producible 7/10, ship* — The concept's central factual claim is wrong and every Uzbek viewer knows it: laser-engraving stalls in every Tashkent underpass and mall will put O'g'iloy on a keychain in five minutes for ~25 000 so'm. The rack of Russian names proves nothing except that you filmed a pre-printed rack. The top comment will be "gravirovka qildirsangiz bo'ladi" and the scarcity proof — the entire "whyItSpreads" mechanism — collapses in public, with the brand cast as either ignorant or dishonest. The caption doubles down into something falsifiable and slightly insulting: a woman who lived 74 years in Uzbekistan has had her name written on a passport, a pension book, a mahalla list and her grandchildren's birth certificates. "Ismi hech qayerda yozilmagan" is not poetry to an Uzbek audience, it is a claim, and it is false.
  - Fix: Cut the Higgsfield shot entirely and replace 0:14–0:19 with a second phone beat — a laser-engraving stall, the 20-second version: hand points at the "gravirovka" sign, vendor says the machine can't do ʻ / or quotes 30 000 so'm and 2 days. That single swap does three things at once: it removes the only AI frame (and every AI tell with it), it pre-empts the comment that kills the premise ("just get it engraved"), and it turns the video into a 100% phone-shot piece that two people can finish by lunch. If the AI orbit must stay for the "we used Higgsfield" box: generate 4–6 takes tonight, not tomorrow, and expect to throw away most of them.
- *risk-conversion 4/10, rework* — The entire engine is a fact the audience can disprove in four seconds, and the comment section that disproves it will be uglier than the video. "Buvim 74 yil yashadi. Ismi hech qayerda yozilmagan" and the caption's "bu uning ismi yozilgan birinchi narsa boʻldi" are simply false: her passport, her pension book, her mahalla paperwork and her children's birth certificates all carry Oʻgʻiloy, and any Chorsu stall will laser-engrave any name onto a blank tag for 20-30k soʻm while you wait — engraving is not exotic in Tashkent, it is the stall next to the one you filmed. So the first reply is "buvingizning pasportida yozilgan-ku" and the second is "gravirovka qildirsangiz boʻlardi," and the brand is now arguing with its own audience about whether its premise was staged. Worse, the shot list picks the disproof fight on ethnic ground: Dima, Nastya, Sasha, Kristina, Dasha spin past and Oʻgʻiloy, Xurshidabegim, Bibisora are absent, published the same week the Senate's Latin-alphabet vote has the language question already inflamed. That reads to half the audience as warm national feeling and to the other half as "russkie imena est, nashih net" resentment bait, and a gift brand does not want to be the venue for that thread. Then two smaller ones that are each independently expensive: the hook is an off-camera question answered by one spoken word with "No text yet" on screen — roughly half of Reels views are muted, so the premise is inaudible to half the test pool and the first 1.5 seconds are a spinning rack with no face; and the vendor is a real, identifiable trader whose unposed refusal is cut as the antagonist beat of a commercial, which needs a signed release, not a verbal "permission asked," because if she later objects the video is unpublishable and the shoot is dead.
  - Fix: Narrow the claim from the world to the rack, and put it on screen as text in the first second. Cut "Ismi hech qayerda yozilmagan" and the caption's "first thing with her name on it" entirely; replace with "Tayyor breloqlarda buvimning ismi yoʻq" ("ready-made keychains don't have my grandmother's name") — true, unfalsifiable, still stings, and it stops being about ethnicity because the enemy becomes the factory's shortlist rather than whose names got printed. Burn that line in as white text at 0:00.3 so the mute half gets the premise before the vendor speaks, and get a one-page release signed at the kiosk before the phone comes out.

**«O'ZIM YOZDIM» DEB ALDADIM** *(native-mischief, mean 5.3)*

- *realism 4/10, rework* — The hook detonates the only bomb in the video. By telling the audience in frame one «I told my sister I wrote this myself», you hand them the punchline before the setup, which means the entire middle — including the three seconds of held silence the concept calls "the frame everyone stops on" — is dead air spent watching a stranger be sincere about something the viewer already knows is a bit. The Songfinch silence works because the viewer shares the recipient's belief; here the viewer is in on it from 0:00, so 0:14–0:30 is a promise-fulfilment wait, not tension, and that is exactly where a 45-second Uzbek-dialogue video with no visual gag bleeds out. Worse, everything that survives the bleed is text-carried: muted, second one is a woman with her hand over her mouth (the single most saturated opener in CIS short-form) plus a full sentence of Uzbek Latin to read. So the concept simultaneously spoils its own retention arc and locks its joke inside one written language, which amputates the KZ/KG/RU diaspora half of the only pool big enough to produce a million. And the reveal itself — "I made her cry with a lie, then laughed, and the proof photo was fake too" — is the most-done reveal on the internet in 2026 and picks a comment fight a gift brand cannot win: the loud half of that thread is "you humiliated your opa to advertise a bot", which poisons the sincerity that the 1 October / to'y / 8 March money actually runs on.
  - Fix: Do not spoil the lie at 0:00. Open with a neutral burned line («opamga qo'shiq qildim») over her already-reacting face, play it completely straight as a sincere reaction video for the first ~10 seconds, and drop the twist line («...va men unga "bir hafta o'zim yozdim" dedim») as the turn at ~0:10. The lie becomes the reveal instead of the premise, the held silence regains its tension, and the first half still works as the proven sincere-reaction unit if the joke half underperforms.
- *producible 8/10, ship* — The entire 40-second middle of this video rests on one Higgsfield asset — a photoreal still of the presenter in a studio, generated from ONE selfie as @image_1 — and the single human who has to be fooled by it is his own sister. Soul ID needs 20+ photos and ~5 minutes of training to lock identity; a single-image reference is the weak path, and reviewers already say exact face fidelity across generations is "harder than the marketing suggests." You are asking that weak path to survive the hardest possible test: the viewer sees his real, live face at 0:03 and the generated version of it five seconds later, and that viewer has known the face for twenty years. Uncanny-valley detection is strongest on intimate faces. If she says "AI ku bu" at 0:08 instead of "voy," there is no prank, no 40 seconds of belief, no reveal, no video — and you cannot reshoot it, because she now knows. One un-testable generation gates the whole shoot day.
  - Fix: Do not generate his face. Generate the studio still with the subject turned away or obscured — back of head at the condenser mic, headphones on, hands on the pop filter, one warm backlight, face out of view or in silhouette. Identity is then carried by the haircut and jacket, which one selfie renders fine and a sister cannot falsify.
