# Instagram trend research — the evidence behind the playbook

**Frozen at 2026-09-16.** This is the raw research that produced
[`01-instagram-launch-playbook.md`](01-instagram-launch-playbook.md). It is corrected by adding, not by
editing: if a finding here turns out to be wrong, append the correction with its date rather than
rewriting the line, so the playbook's reasoning stays legible.

Every finding carries the confidence its researcher assigned it. **Findings marked ⚠️ *unverified* are
inferences the agent could not confirm against a source** — they were deliberately kept rather than
quietly dropped, and nothing in the playbook should rest on one alone.

Seven sweeps ran in parallel, plus a free-model brainstorm whose notes are at the end.

## What makes a Reel reach a million in 2026 — ranking, hooks, suppression

> **Optimise the Bayram Reels for DM SENDS at 45-60 seconds — not for likes and not as sub-10s loops — and stop worrying about the AI video tool: correctly labelled AI content is explicitly not demoted, and the only real AI penalty (live since 31 August 2026) hits unlabelled AI-generated PERSONA accounts, not human-run accounts that use AI tools.**

### Findings

**Instagram's stated top-3 ranking signals are still watch time, likes-per-reach and sends-per-reach — with likes weighted more for follower reach and SENDS weighted more for non-follower (recommendation) reach. The most recent official-sounding wording I could trace is Adam Mosseri, 22 January 2025: "The top three signals that matter most for ranking are watch time, likes and sends." No newer official restatement surfaced for Jul–Sep 2026; every 2026 guide is still re-quoting the Jan 2025 line.**  
likely — Quote reported across dataslayer.ai, blckalpaca.at, highstyle.ai, socialync.io, all dating it 22 Jan 2025 and attributing it to Mosseri via @creators / Social Media Today. I could NOT reach a primary Instagram/Meta page carrying the quote (creators.instagram.com/blog/instagram-ranking-explained = 404, help.instagram.com/313829416281232 returned an empty shell).  
→ *So what:* Write every Bayram Reel to be SENT, not liked. The KPI for a 1M-view attempt is sends-per-reach, and the edit must contain one explicit "I need to send this to X" beat — for a birthday-song bot that beat is a named recipient ("send this to the friend whose birthday you always forget").

**The widely repeated "a send is worth 3–5x a like" / "1 DM share = 15 likes" multipliers are NOT confirmed by Meta. Multiple 2026 write-ups explicitly call them community-derived estimates; one source attributes the 3–5x to Buffer's own April 2026 research rather than to Instagram.**  
⚠️ *unverified* — dataslayer.ai states "Community-derived estimates put the multiplier at 3-5x over likes for non-follower reach, though Meta has not confirmed an exact ratio." socialync.io attributes 3–5x to Buffer April 2026 research. No Meta source found.  
→ *So what:* Use the direction (sends > likes for cold reach) in creative decisions, but never put the 3–5x number in a deck or a client promise. If you need a number, measure your own sends-per-reach in Instagram Insights.

**Saves are a real Explore signal but are NOT in Instagram's named top three. Comments are also absent from the named top three.**  
likely — Consistent across dataslayer.ai, socialync.io, orangemonke.com (2026 updates) — all reconcile "saves matter" with "Mosseri's named top three were watch time, likes per reach, sends per reach." No primary source.  
→ *So what:* Stop designing Reels around "save this for later" CTAs. For Bayram, a save is a dead end; a send is a lead, because the person receiving the DM is exactly the person who needs a birthday song.

**On the largest recent dataset I could verify, 45–60 seconds is the best-performing Reel length for BRAND accounts in 2026 — not sub-10s loops. Socialinsider analysed ~6M Instagram Reels from brand accounts, January–June 2026: engagement rate by bracket 0–30s 0.28%, 30–45s 0.30%, 45–60s 0.35%, 60–90s 0.30%, 90–120s 0.30%, 120–180s 0.33%, 180s+ 0.15%. Median views: 1–30s 4,700; 30–45s 8,564; 45–60s 10,374; 60–90s 9,790; 180s+ 4,428. The 45–60s bracket wins both metrics and roughly doubles the median views of sub-30s Reels.**  
**confirmed** — https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-length/ — sample stated as "6M Instagram Reels, coming from brands that had an active presence on Instagram between January-June 2026". Numbers quoted verbatim from the page.  
→ *So what:* Default Bayram Reel length = 45–60s, not a 7s loop. That contradicts the folk wisdom. Build the AI-video script as a 50-second piece with a real arc (problem → build → payoff) rather than a one-gag loop, and only cut to <15s if your own retention curve says the back half is dead.

**Reels longer than 3 minutes fall off a cliff — Socialinsider states they "don't get placed in the Reels tab" and receive follower-only visibility. Separately, Instagram raised the Reels limit to 3 minutes and made up-to-3-minute Reels eligible for non-follower recommendations and Explore during 2026.**  
likely — socialinsider.io (length study, Jan–Jun 2026) for the >3min cliff; later.com (updated 21 Apr 2026) and napoleoncat.com Instagram-updates tracker for 3-minute Reels reaching non-followers. ### Formats to copy

- THE NAMED-RECIPIENT PAYOFF (45-55s). Hook (0-1.5s): open on a phone screen already playing a personalised birthday song with the recipient's name sung out loud — no intro, the name lands in the first second. - THE COMPARISON VERDICT (40-60s). Hook: "₽0 birthday card vs a song with her name in it" on frame one. Structure: side-by-side, hard cut every 2-3s, clear verdict at the end. The reveal moment is the share trigger. Keep the reveal at roughly 60-70% through, never the final frame, so the loop tail carries into a replay.
- THE MUTED-READABLE EXPLAINER (45-60s). Every beat burned in as on-screen text inside the 1080x1320 safe area, the generated song as the ORIGINAL audio track so it gets its own audio page. Works at ~50% mute-watch rates and creates a second discovery surface other accounts can post to.
- THE TRIAL-REEL HOOK LADDER (any length, 5-10 variants). Not a video format but a publishing format: same 45s body, 5-10 different first-3-seconds, all shipped as Trial Reels to non-followers only. Read skip rate and sends at ~24h, then re-publish the single winner as a normal Reel. This is how a <1000-follower account buys data instead of guessing.

### Formats to copy

- LINE-BY-LINE PERFORMANCE CUT (the core Bayram format). Structure: 1 hero still generated in Soul → 8–12 clips of 5–8s each, one per lyric line, each generated in Lipsync Studio (InfiniteTalk or Speak 2.0) against that line's audio slice, each with a DIFFERENT camera preset (Glam → Arc Left → Hero Cam → 360 Orbit → Crane Up). - EARTH ZOOM-OUT REVERSE OPENER. Structure: screenshot the frame you want to open on → Higgsfield Effects > Earth Zoom Out → generate (~2 min) → REVERSE the clip in the editor and stitch it in front of your real footage, so it zooms IN from space onto the subject. Hook = the scale shift in the first 1.5s. Length: 3–5s as an opener on a 30–60s video. Source: documented step-by-step in two TikTok creator tutorials (@ai.for.real.life/video/7529203311254195511, @dollarmoovies/video/7532276173812796727). Note: this preset is well past peak and now reads as AI — use the MECHANIC (scale shift in first 2s) rather than the literal Earth Zoom.
- SPLIT-SCREEN BEFORE/AFTER (Higgsfield 'Living Paintings' structure, generalised). Structure: original/static image on the bottom half, animated version on the top half, both playing at once. Hook = the moment the top half starts moving while the bottom stays frozen. Length: 6–10s. Source: higgsfield.ai/blog/how-to-make-viral-videos-viral-presets — "A split-screen format: the original painting below, your animated reimagining above." For Bayram: user's real submitted photo below, the animated singing version above.
- BEFORE & AFTER TRANSFORMATION. Structure: state A → hard transition kept to "2 to 3 seconds" → state B. Hook = visible progress. Length: under 15s. Source: higgsfield.ai/blog/Product-Videos-TikTok-Reels-Without-Filming, format #2.
- MINIMAL LOOP. Structure: a single seamless motion that returns to its first frame, "keep loops under seven seconds." Hook = the viewer not noticing the loop point and watching twice (drives hold rate). Length: <7s. Source: same article, format #10.
- REACTION CUT. Structure: stimulus shot → cut to face reaction. Hook = "human emotion is the strongest hook." Length: 5–10s, two clips. Source: same article, format #5. For Bayram: the birthday person's animated face reacting to their own song.
- POPCORN 8-FRAME STORYBOARD → 8 CLIPS. Structure: one prompt expands into an 8-frame sequence with character identity, lighting and atmosphere locked across all frames; each frame then becomes a video clip. Hook = the first frame. Length: 8 × 5s = 40s of consistent narrative. Source: higgsfield.ai help-center Popcorn page (up to 8 frames, up to 4 image references, Auto or Manual mode).
- RECAST TEMPLATE REUSE. Structure: one master 15s performance video, shot once, with the birthday person's face swapped in per order via Recast (gesture tracking + voice clone + dubbing preserved). Hook = whatever you designed into the master once. Length: up to the master's length. Source: higgsfield.ai/apps/recast.

## How AI products have actually gone viral in short form

> **Stop making ads and start harvesting customers' phone footage: the one commercially-proven format for a personalised-song product is the customer-shot reaction clip where you refuse to cut the three seconds of silence before the recipient reacts — Songfinch went $0 to $250k in 30 days on exactly that, and everything else (name-in-comments, AI-mispronounces-Uzbek-names, a public Bayram sound) is volume strategy layered on top of it.**

### Findings

**The single best-documented format for a personalised-gift product is the CUSTOMER-SHOT reaction video, not a brand-shot one. Songfinch (custom songs, direct Bayram analogue) built its TikTok channel on "authentic and raw videos obtained from customers" of real people hearing their song, with captions added for context — and went $0 → $250,000 revenue in the first 30 days on TikTok, 3,500 conversions in the first three months. Agency-side numbers on the same creative library: +206% Meta conversions, +280% conversion rate, 4.0–4.82 YouTube ROAS, +333% YoY revenue.**  
likely — TikTok for Business case study "Songfinch — North America" (tiktok.com/business/en-US/inspiration/songfinch-north-america, surfaced in search; direct fetch timed out) + Pilothouse Digital case study (pilothouse.co/clients-success/songfinch-case-study). NOTE: this campaign is from ~2021–2022, well before the July–Sept 2026 window.  
→ *So what:* Do not film the reaction yourself. Build a pipeline that asks every paying Bayram customer for the 20-second phone clip of the recipient hearing the song, with an incentive (free second song / refund), then re-cut it with a caption card. The customer's shaky vertical phone video IS the ad format; a produced version of it measurably underperforms.

**Reaction-gift videos die when they read as scripted, and the editing rule that separates real from staged is: do NOT cut the silence. The ~3-second stunned pause before the recipient reacts is the payload; cutting it for pacing is the main tell. Other tells: locked tripod instead of handheld, brand product visible before the emotion, and commenters interrogating the creator's gifting history. Reported consequence: skip rates on "surprise" reveals now rival pre-roll ads.**  
likely — influencers-time.com, "Gift Exchange Reaction Videos: A Brief Brands Can't Fake", published 16/07/2026 — in window. Qualitative trade piece, no view statistics.  
→ *So what:* Bayram's edit template: handheld, phone speaker audible (not a clean audio bed), hold the silence between the first line of the song and the recipient's face, never cut to a UI screen before the reaction lands. Show the bot only in the last 3 seconds or in the caption/comment.

**The highest-leverage structural choice for an emotional AI gift product is that the reaction lands EARLY, not at the end. Native-feeling short-form in 2026 follows: native-style hook in seconds 0–3, tension seconds 4–10, proof/demo 11–20, CTA in the last 5–10. 47% of a Meta video campaign's value is delivered in the first three seconds; 71% of TikTok users decide inside those same three seconds; ~36% of viewers scroll the moment a video "feels like an ad".**  
likely — adlibrary.com "Reels Ads 2026" and digitalapplied.com "Instagram Reels Ads 2026: Creative Strategy Guide"; adstellar.ai "Hook Rate For Video Ads" (all 2026-dated marketing-industry blogs, not primary platform research).  
→ *So what:* Open cold on the recipient's face already reacting (or the first mispronounced syllable), then rewind to "I made my mum a song with her name in it". Never open with the Telegram bot screen. Bayram's product reveal belongs at second 15+.

**Polished production is a measurable liability: native-style assets (handheld, natural light, lo-fi edit) lose 25–30% of viewers by second 2 versus 45–50% for studio-produced assets with the SAME offer, and achieve lower CPMs and higher completion.**  
likely — adlibrary.com / digitalapplied.com Reels-2026 creative guides (2026). Vendor-side aggregate, no methodology published.  
→ *So what:* Ban the following from Bayram creative: motion-graphic logo stings, voiceover recorded on a good mic, colour grading, any b-roll not shot on a phone. Shoot everything on a Tashkent phone in a kitchen/car/dastarxon.

**Faceless multi-account volume, not a single hero video, is how AI apps actually got distribution. Cal AI ran 12+ TikTok accounts and 1,000+ videos; the accounts were wildly unequal — @cal_ai_ 137 videos → 7.5M views, @getcalai 281 videos → 2.1M (top video 5.2M), @calai.track 176 videos → 54.7K views. likely — growwithplutus.com/blog/cal-ai-app-tiktok-strategy (article dated Apr 22 2025, page re-dated Sep 8 2026). Single-source third-party scrape; Cal AI itself reports 15M downloads / ~$30–40M ARR (Starter Story, getlatka).  
→ *So what:* Plan for a portfolio, not a campaign: 4–6 Bayram accounts (different personas — a bride, a son, a teacher, a "bayram" content account), expect 1–2 to carry everything and 2–3 to die. Budget the dead ones in advance instead of treating them as failure.

**The largest AI-app distribution machine on record was paid-per-view faceless creators, not influencers. ### Formats to copy

- BLIND REACTION IN THE CAR (6–15s, vertical, phone-filmed). Songfinch's highest-performing and most-copied unit. Shot 1 (0–1s): tight on a face, headphones or car speaker, no product visible. Shot 2 (1–4s): the song plays and the NAME lands in the lyric — the face breaks. Shot 3 (4–6s): cut to the giver laughing/crying. Caption carries the offer, not the voiceover. Hook = the face, not a line. Localise by making the lyric moment the Uzbek name pronounced correctly.
- "THE PERFECT GIFT DOESN'T EXI—" PATTERN INTERRUPT (8–12s). Songfinch's own TikTok: text-on-screen starts the cliché, cuts itself off mid-word, then reveals. Hook is a broken sentence in the first 1.5 seconds. Uzbek version: "Eng zo'r sovg'a yo'q de— ..." then the song starts.
- THE MISPRONUNCIATION A/B (10–15s). Nobody in the category runs this because nobody else can win it. Shot 1 (0–3s): generic AI generator sings a mangled "Muhayyo"/"Gʻayrat" — the joke lands instantly for any Uzbek viewer. Shot 2 (3–6s): "Bizniki-chi?" Shot 3 (6–12s): the same name, sung right. Hook = the wrong pronunciation, i.e. the enemy on screen before the product.
- THE PRICE STACK (6–8s, static or slow push). Three cards: torT 200 000 / gullar 40 000 / qo'shiq 15 000, third one lands with the song audio under it. Hook is the biggest number first. This is the 'cheaper than a taxi' framing done visually rather than as a claim.
- FREE-PREVIEW SCREEN RECORD (12–20s). Copied from MelodyBolt/SongFor's funnel, which no CIS bot runs. Shot 1: typing a name into the Telegram bot. Shot 2: 30-second preview arrives in the chat. Shot 3: the Payme button, pressed. Hook = "To'lamasdan turib eshiting" in the first second. Doubles as scam-proofing against the July 2026 fake-greeting fraud wave.
- TAXI POV (8–12s). Uzbekistan-native: filmed from the back seat, fare on the meter reads ~15 000, cut to the song playing on the same phone. Hook = the meter. Ties the price to the one thing every Tashkent viewer paid for today.
- GIFT-GIVER TESTIMONIAL WITH CONTEXT CAPTION (15–25s). Pilothouse's stated Meta variant: raw customer clip, but with a typed caption supplying the relationship and occasion ("for my mum's 60th") because the footage alone doesn't explain itself. ## The craft layer — hooks, captions, cadence, sendability

> **Design every Reel for one metric — sends per reach, the strongest 2026 ranking signal — by making the viewer think of one specific named person within the first 3 seconds, and stop treating hashtags as distribution: Instagram hard-capped them at 5 on 19 Dec 2025.**

### Findings

**Instagram hard-capped hashtags at 5 per post/Reel, announced 19 Dec 2025 by @Creators and Adam Mosseri; extra tags are ignored or publishing is blocked. Mosseri: "While I know it can be tempting to use more, a few specific tags actually perform better than a long list of generic ones. Quality over quantity is key."**  
**confirmed** — Social Media Today, "Instagram Implements New Limits on Hashtag Use", 18 Dec 2025 (https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-implements-new-limits-on-hashtag-use/808309/); Digital Trends "Instagram now limits you to five hashtags per post"; Later hashtag guide 2026  
→ *So what:* Any 2023-era "30 hashtags" template is now actively harmful. Ship exactly 5 per Reel and treat them as classification labels, not reach. Build a fixed 5-slot template: 1 branded (#bayram or your handle tag), 1 category (#sovga / #подарок), 1 local (#toshkent or #uzbekistan), 1 occasion (#tugilgankun), 1 format tag that rotates.

**Mosseri has said on the record that hashtags do not meaningfully drive reach — "they do help us understand what a post is about … but in general, no, I wouldn't try to think of hashtags as a way to get more distribution" (Feb 2025). Instagram's own Dec 2025 line is that fewer targeted tags "can improve both your content's performance and people's experience."**  
**confirmed** — Mosseri quote relayed in Social Media Today (Dec 2025) and Kontentino Q&A; note the underlying Mosseri statement is Feb 2025, older than the July–Sept 2026 window  
→ *So what:* Move the discovery budget out of hashtags and into caption keywords, on-screen text and the name field. Do not A/B hashtag sets expecting reach deltas; spend that testing effort on hooks instead.

**Sends per reach (DM forwards ÷ reach) is the strongest 2026 ranking signal for reaching non-followers on Reels; secondary sources put a send at roughly 3–5x a like for distribution, with more aggressive Reels-specific estimates near 15x. Published benchmark bands: <0.5% send rate = capped at followers; 0.5–1% average; 1–2% strong, Explore pickup begins; 3%+ = 7.5–10x follower reach.**  
likely — CreatorLane HQ, "Instagram Sends Per Reach — The 2026 Ranking Signal Decoded" (https://creatorlanehq.com/blog/instagram-sends-per-reach-2026); corroborated directionally by Sprout Social and Hootsuite 2026 algorithm guides. The multipliers and band thresholds are agency estimates, NOT Meta-published numbers  
→ *So what:* Make send rate the single north-star metric in the content log, not views or likes. Track sends/reach per Reel from day one and kill any format that sits under 0.5% after three attempts. This is also the perfect signal alignment for a gift product: the product IS a send.

**Documented sendability triggers (the reasons a viewer forwards to ONE named person): (1) niche in-jokes that signal identity, (2) hyper-relatable observations with a named recipient archetype, (3) single-screenshot wisdom, (4) POV scenarios, (5) polarizing takes, (6) useful-save-that-is-also-a-send, (7) fascination loops. Underlying psychology: sharing is identity expression — a widely cited NYT sharing study found 68% share "to give others a sense of what they're interested in"; Berger's social-currency levers are affiliation, conversation, utility, advocacy, information, identity.**  
likely — CreatorLane HQ sends-per-reach piece (7 patterns); NYT sharing study 68% figure as relayed by multiple psychology-of-sharing write-ups (original study is c.2011 — OLD, treat as directional); Jonah Berger social currency  
→ *So what:* Our whole distribution is trigger #2: a Reel must make the viewer think of one specific person within 3 seconds. Write every hook to name a recipient archetype in Uzbek/Russian — "onangiz uchun", "eng yaqin dugonangizga", "твоей маме" — rather than describing the product. The song-for-one-person mechanic IS trigger #4 (POV) + #1 (identity) stacked.

**A verbal or on-screen CTA at the end of a Reel asking for the share is reported to roughly double share rate; open loops (viewer wants to watch a friend's reaction to the reveal) are an independent share driver.**  
⚠️ *unverified* — Hootsuite Reels-for-business 2026 guide and Socialync "Instagram shares algorithm" 2026 guide; the "double" figure is a creator-consensus claim with no published study behind it  
→ *So what:* End every Reel with an explicit named-recipient CTA in the viewer's language plus a one-beat reveal they'd want a reaction to (the finished song playing while the recipient's face changes). Never use generic "share this" — always "send this to the person whose birthday you keep forgetting".

**Hook taxonomy that still works in 2026: instant-promise, contradiction, before-and-after (visual, no text needed), list-with-a-twist, visual confession, price tag/specific number, pattern interrupt. Reported 3-second retention by hook class: pattern interrupt 72–84%, curiosity gap 65–78%, direct question 58–72%, bold claim 55–70%, problem 50–65%, social proof 45–60%.**  
likely — FlowCast, "Instagram Reels Hooks: 7 Formats That Still Work in 2026", 21 May 2026 (https://flowcast.space/blog/instagram-reels-hooks-that-work-in-2026) for the taxonomy and example lines; Retensis "Instagram Reels Skip Rate Benchmarks 2026" for the retention bands. Retention bands are vendor benchmarks, not Meta data  
→ *So what:* Default to pattern interrupt + before/after for us: open on the recipient's face mid-reaction (no text), then cut to the phone screen. Avoid social-proof hooks ("1000 people used this") — lowest band and we have no proof yet.

**Burnt out by Sept 2026: "wait for it" overlays, generic curiosity gaps ("broad curiosity feels spammy now, specific curiosity feels valuable"), "3 things I wish I knew before I started X", guru-voice "stop doing X" commands, and any line copied from a viral-hooks PDF. Negative/contradiction hooks still work but only when specific and true.**  
likely — FlowCast (May 2026) names "3 things I wish I knew" as tired; Tweetangels "Instagram Reels Viral Hooks 2026" and Blitzcut "Best TikTok Hooks for 2026" both call "wait for it" outdated; Kontentino/OpusClip on generic vs specific curiosity. All are agency blogs, no platform data  
→ *So what:* Blacklist in the hook doc: "wait for it", "POV: you..." (generic), "stop doing X", "3 things I wish I knew". Whitelist: contradiction with a number, and direct address naming a recipient. Re-audit the blacklist monthly — hooks decay in ~6 months.

**The first frame is the thumbnail by default and it is usually motion blur. Cover is 1080x1920 but crops to ~3:4 (1080x1440) on the profile grid; keep faces/text in the centre 60% vertically, nothing critical in the top or bottom 20%. Cover title 3–5 words max, 48–60pt minimum at 1080px width, high contrast, face on one side with text on the opposite side. Covers can be changed later with no loss of views or comments.**  
likely — Hypefury 2026 Reels thumbnail guide; JW Toolbox "Instagram Reel Cover Size 2026: 1080x1920 + Safe Zone"; wavegen.ai reel size 2026  
→ *So what:* Never ship a Reel without a hand-picked cover. Standardise one cover template: recipient's face top-left, 3-word Uzbek title right, same font every time so the grid reads as a series. Because covers are editable post-hoc, re-cover the top 3 Reels weekly and test.

**Up to ~50% of viewers drop in the first 3 seconds; skip rate under 20% at 3s is the target, 20–30% healthy, over 40% means the hook is broken. A 10s Reel at 80% retention beats a 60s Reel at 30%. Mosseri's own published Reels advice: strong hook, captions on, audio on, non-watermarked original, 3 minutes or less.**  
likely — Retensis skip-rate benchmarks 2026; Hootsuite Instagram algorithm 2026 guide relaying Mosseri's Reels checklist (no exact date given for the Mosseri Reel)  
→ *So what:* Cap our daily format at 10–15 seconds until send rate is proven. Measure 3-second skip rate in Insights on every post and treat >40% as an automatic reshoot of the first frame only, not the whole video.

**Caption: 2,200-char limit, truncated at ~125 characters in feed. First 125 chars must carry the hook and the keyword. Reported optimum for comments is 150–220 words (product/promo posts peak lower, 50–150 words). Instagram indexes caption text, bio, name field, alt text and on-screen/spoken words in Reels for search.**  
likely — boomp.net "Instagram Caption Best Practices 2026"; Aibrify optimal caption length 2026 (the "50,000+ posts analysed / 150–220 words / +40%" figure is that vendor's own claim, unaudited); Later Instagram SEO guide; Toptal Creator Instagram SEO 2026  
→ *So what:* Write captions in two layers: first 125 chars = Uzbek keyword hook ("Tug'ilgan kunga sovg'a: ismi bilan qo'shiq"), then 60–120 words of context, then the send-CTA, then 5 hashtags. Keep it short — this is a promo product, not an educational carousel.

**Alt text is keyword-indexed by Instagram and also feeds Google Image search; keep under 125 chars, descriptive, no stuffing. If left blank Instagram auto-generates a generic and often wrong description. Most accounts skip it entirely.**  
likely — SocialKit "Instagram Alt Text: Accessibility and Hidden SEO Wins"; Toptal Creator Instagram SEO 2026; Later Instagram SEO  
→ *So what:* Add alt text to every static/carousel post with the Uzbek + Russian keyword pair. Zero cost, and nobody in the local market does it.

**Uzbekistan market size: DataReportal Digital 2026 (published 8 Nov 2025) reports Instagram reaching 14.1M people in Uzbekistan = 37.9% of population, +4.15M YoY (+41.7%), adult gender split 65.5% male / 32.5% female; 33.1M internet users (89.0%). NapoleonCat's July 2026 estimate is higher at 17.42M users (49.4% of population), 67.5% male, largest cohort 18–24 at 5.8M. Telegram penetration in Uzbekistan is reported as the world's highest per-capita at ~70%.**  
**confirmed** — https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-uzbekistan (8 Nov 2025); https://stats.napoleoncat.com/instagram-users-in-uzbekistan/ (July 2026); Telegram figure from Business of Apps / HackMD 2026 country round-ups — the ~70% number is secondary, not Telegram-published  
→ *So what:* Two hard planning facts: (a) the IG audience skews strongly male (65–67%), so gift-for-her framing aimed at a male buyer is the primary persona, not the reverse; (b) Telegram is near-universal, so the IG→Telegram deep link is a low-friction handoff, not a drop-off — the bot is where the audience already lives.

**Language split of Instagram content in Uzbekistan is roughly even, ~35–36% of posts in Uzbek and ~35–36% in Russian; agency guidance is Russian for Tashkent B2C, Uzbek for the regions and younger audiences.**

### Formats to copy

- THE REACTION CUT (10-14s, daily driver). Frame 1: a person's face mid-reaction, no text, no logo, no talking head. Cut at 2s to the phone screen showing the song title with their name in it. Cut back to the face. End card 2s: 'Kimga yuborasiz?' Hook is visual-only — it works with sound off and needs no translation. Cover = the reaction face, 3-word Uzbek title on the opposite side.
- THE CONTRADICTION OPENER (12-18s). Hook line on screen for 1.5s: 'Gul 3 kunda so'liydi. Bu qo'shiq qolаdi.' / 'Цветы завянут за 3 дня.' Then the product in one continuous shot. Contradiction hooks are the one classic taxonomy entry FlowCast (May 2026) still lists as live, and it maps exactly onto the gift category's real objection.
- THE NAMED-RECIPIENT ARCHETYPE (8-12s). One Reel = one recipient type: 'Onangiz uchun', 'Eng yaqin dugonangizga', 'Akangizga'. The archetype IS the hook and IS the send instruction — this is sendability trigger #2, the highest-leverage one for a one-to-one gift product. Run the same format across 7 archetypes = a week of content from one shoot.
- THE NUMBERED DAILY SERIES ('Kun N — bugun kimning tug'ilgan kuni?', 15s). Same set, same font, same slot every evening; only the person and the song change. The number is the return hook, the template kills the daily creative decision, and the consistent cover turns the profile grid into a visible series.
- THE PRICE-TAG HOOK (10s). Open with the number on screen, nothing else: 'X so'm. 1 qo'shiq. 1 odam.' FlowCast lists price tag as a live 2026 format; specificity is what separates working hooks from burnt-out ones in every source we found.
- THE BEFORE/AFTER SPLIT (8s, zero text). Left frame: the generic gift everyone buys. Right frame: the song playing. Pure visual, no on-screen copy — the only hook class that survives the language split (uz/ru roughly 35/36% each) with one asset.
- THE OPEN-LOOP REVEAL (15-20s). Show the song being made but never the recipient's reaction; cut to black on the moment of handing over. The viewer sends it because they want to watch a friend's reaction to the reveal — open loops are named as an independent share driver, distinct from the CTA.
