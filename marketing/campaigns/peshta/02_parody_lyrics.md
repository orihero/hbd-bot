## 1. Metrical, Cadence & Audio Grid Architecture

### 1.1 Classical Barmoq Vazni ($4 + 4 + 5$ Turoq)

In Uzbek prosody, Turkic folk and modern pop poetry operate under **Barmoq vazni** (syllabic verse). Unlike quantitative meters (Aruz), Barmoq relies on two immutable structural principles:
1. **Isometric Syllable Count**: Every line within a strophe must maintain the identical number of syllables. In Hamdam Sobirov's "Peshta", every verse line contains **exactly 13 syllables**.
2. **Caesura Division (Turoq)**: Syllables are grouped into fixed rhythmic clusters separated by micro-pauses (caesuras). The 13-syllable meter in modern Uzbek dance-pop uses a tripartite division:
   $$\text{Line} = \text{Turoq}_1 \, (4 \text{ syllables}) + \text{Turoq}_2 \, (4 \text{ syllables}) + \text{Turoq}_3 \, (5 \text{ syllables}) = 13 \text{ syllables}$$

The $4+4+5$ structure provides a balanced initial double-stride ($4+4$) followed by an extended, syncopated 5-syllable cadence ($5$) that leads directly into the bar line.

```
+-------------------+-------------------+-------------------------+
| Turoq 1 (4 syl)   | Turoq 2 (4 syl)   | Turoq 3 (5 syl)         |
| 4 Eighth Notes    | 4 Eighth Notes    | Syncopated Flourish     |
+-------------------+-------------------+-------------------------+
| Kar - ta - sin - da | pu - li  tug - ab | me - ni   iz - la - di  |
| Gul  kel - tir - sam | ik - ki  kun - da | so' - lib qo - la - di  |
| To'y - xo - na - da | mik - ro - fon - ni | ol - dim  qo' - lim - ga |
| Yil  bo' - yi  men | sov - g'a ku - tib | or - zu   qil - gan - dim|
+-------------------+-------------------+-------------------------+
```

## 3. Syllabic & Linguistic Verification Engine

### 3.1 The Vowel-Syllable Invariant Theorem ($N_{\text{vowels}} = 13$)

In Uzbek phonology and standard Latin orthography, syllable structure obeys the fundamental syllabic equation:
$$N_{\text{syllables}} = N_{\text{vowels}}$$
Every syllable contains exactly one vowel nucleus. Consequently, an uncompromised mathematical test for whether any line of verse fulfills the **13-syllable Barmoq vazni** requirement is that the count of vowel phonemes in standard orthography equals **13**.

### 3.2 Uzbek Orthographic Vowel Rules

Uzbek Latin orthography recognizes six vowel letters representing the vowel phonemes:
1. **`a`** (e.g., *karta*, *bayram*)
2. **`e`** (e.g., *meni*, *keldi*)
3. **`i`** (e.g., *puli*, *jiyan*)
4. **`o`** (e.g., *sovush*, *bosh*)
5. **`u`** (e.g., *tugab*, *yurak*)
6. **`o'`** or **`oʻ`** (e.g., *qo'shiq*, *to'yxona*, *ko'zi*)

*Rule*: The letter combination `o'` (or `oʻ`) represents a single rounded mid-back vowel phoneme `/ɵ/` or `/o/`. The inverted comma or apostrophe is an orthographic diacritic modifier, **not** a separate syllable.

### 3.3 Treatment of Semi-Vowels, Diphthongoids & Tutuq Belgisi

1. **Semi-vowels (`y`, `v`) and Diphthongoids**:
   - Combinations such as `ay`, `oy`, `uy`, `ey`, `av`, `ov` consist of a vowel plus a consonantal glide (`/j/` or `/w/`). They are phonologically and orthographically categorized as closed monosyllables:
     - `Bay-ram` = 2 syllables (Vowels: `a`, `a`)
     - `to'y` = 1 syllable (Vowel: `o'`)
     - `kuy-ib` = 2 syllables (Vowels: `u`, `i`)
     - `yay-ra-di` = 3 syllables (Vowels: `a`, `a`, `i`)
     - `pay-poq` = 2 syllables (Vowels: `a`, `o`)
2. **Tutuq Belgisi (Hamza / Apostrophe `ʼ`)**:
   - In words of Arabic origin (`maʼno`, `sanʼat`, `sheʼr`), the tutuq belgisi marks a glottal stop.
   - While `sheʼr` is orthographically 1 syllable (1 vowel `e`), vocalists frequently expand it into two moras (*"she-ir"*).
   - *Design Decision*: To prevent vocal ambiguity or automated parser conflicts, all parody lyrics intentionally use unambiguous native Uzbek vocabulary (`qo'shiq`, `tarona`, `navo`) rather than hamza-dependent loan words.

### 3.4 Colloquial Contractions in Sung Uzbek

In spoken Uzbek and contemporary pop delivery (exemplified by Hamdam Sobirov's own performances):
- `olib bergan` ($4 \text{ syllables}$) contracts to `obergan` ($3 \text{ syllables}$) — as in *"Men obergan mersedesni"*.
- `qilib qo'ydim` ($4 \text{ syllables}$) contracts to `qipqo'ydim` or `qivordim` ($3 \text{ syllables}$).
- `qilibman` ($3 \text{ syllables}$) contracts to `qipman` ($2 \text{ syllables}$).
- `Telegram` is pronounced naturally as trisyllabic `te-leg-ram` ($3 \text{ syllables}$).

*Standardization Mandate*: In our delivery scripts, every line is written in **exact phonetic-to-orthographic equivalence** so that human vocalists, audio directors, and automated inspection scripts obtain the identical count of 13 syllables.
